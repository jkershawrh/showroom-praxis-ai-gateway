= Track 3: Assess industry evidence

Executives and auditors need more than a successful demo, but different industries care about different failure modes. You will use the competency matrix to evaluate evidence without turning a technical lab into an unsupported compliance claim.

== What you will learn

* Map industry concerns to platform control evidence
* Separate verified, planned, preview, and unsupported capabilities
* Produce a defensible go/no-go assessment

== See: Evidence before claims

image::architecture.svg[Praxis platform evidence spanning clients gateway and model backend,link=self,window=_blank]

Banking teams may prioritize tenant attribution, approved-model routing, and retention. Healthcare providers may emphasize data minimization and controlled egress. Government environments may prioritize authorization boundaries and reproducible configuration. Manufacturing and telecommunications teams may focus on availability and centralized change control.

These motivations overlap, but their policies are not interchangeable. The lab therefore uses scenario profiles rather than claiming a universal industry configuration. Legal, privacy, security, and records teams must approve the target design.

The capability matrix labels each Praxis feature by evidence maturity. A released and tested function can support a hands-on requirement. An experimental demonstration can inform a discussion but cannot silently become a production promise.

The claim registry applies the same discipline to numbers. Hardware estimates and completion time remain unverified until measured in the target environment. Honest uncertainty is stronger evidence than false precision.

== Do: Build an evidence assessment

=== Step 1: Review capability maturity

Read the matrix and identify which capabilities are core, optional, preview, or deferred.

[source,bash,role="execute",subs="attributes+"]
----
cd ~/praxis-lab
sed -n '1,240p' docs/capability-matrix.md
----

Do not promote an experimental Grid or policy-engine example into the core lab without release evidence.

=== Step 2: Inspect the claim registry

List every quantified claim and its verification state.

[source,bash,role="execute",subs="attributes+"]
----
cd ~/praxis-lab
python3 -c 'import yaml; d=yaml.safe_load(open("tests/claim_registry.yaml")); [print(c["id"], c["verified"]) for c in d["claims"]]'
----

Unverified values must remain labeled as estimates rather than guarantees.

=== Step 3: Choose an industry scenario

Choose a scenario, then create a sanitized evidence bundle that survives the lab session. The collector intentionally excludes Secrets, prompts, responses, and access tokens.

[source,bash,role="execute",subs="attributes+"]
----
cd ~/praxis-lab
python3 tools/collect_evidence.py \
  --project {project_name} \
  --scenario "healthcare provider shared AI access"
ls -lh artifacts/praxis-evidence-*.tar.gz
----

Open the generated `assessment.md` and adapt the scenario, missing controls, and decision to your organization. Keep the archive; it is the learner-created deliverable from this lab.

=== Step 4: Run publication safeguards

Execute the tests that prevent unsupported product, architecture, and compliance wording.

[source,bash,role="execute",subs="attributes+"]
----
cd ~/praxis-lab && python3 -m pytest tests/publication tests/contracts -q
----

All safeguards should pass.

=== Verify

Confirm the assessment contains no unconditional compliance assertion.

[source,bash,role="execute",subs="attributes+"]
----
ASSESSMENT=$(find ~/praxis-lab/artifacts -name assessment.md -print | tail -1)
if grep -Eqi 'is compliant|guarantees compliance|certified compliant' "$ASSESSMENT"; then echo RED; else echo GREEN; fi
----

The result should be green.

== Key takeaway

Industry alignment starts with explicit evidence and ownership, not a compliance badge. You leave with a sanitized bundle and assessment that reviewers can refine, while clearly separating what the lab proved from what still requires production validation.
