= Map the customer architecture

The closing governance exercise converts the demonstration into a customer
architecture conversation. It records what was proven, what remains a preview,
and which decisions belong in a proof of concept.

== Customer outcome

* Separate implemented, demonstrated, preview, and target-state capabilities
* Map customer concerns to OpenShift and gateway responsibilities
* Define a bounded next step without making unsupported compliance claims

== Know: Evidence before claims

Different industries emphasize different risks. Banking teams may prioritize
tenant attribution, approved-model routing, and retention. Healthcare teams
may emphasize data minimization and controlled egress. Government environments
may require reproducible configuration and stronger authorization boundaries.
Manufacturing and telecommunications teams may focus on availability and
centralized change control.

These motivations overlap, but the policies are not interchangeable. Legal,
privacy, security, and records teams must approve the target design.

The pinned experience proves a gateway boundary, Secret ownership, an internal
Praxis service, an authenticated learner UI, exact-request correlation, and
controlled OpenShift recovery. It does not prove full distributed tracing,
production authorization, dynamic policy routing, or compliance.

The Praxis Policy Engine to OCSF immutable-ledger path is a contract-ready
preview. The reference adapter can validate versioned fixtures, but the pinned
Praxis build does not yet emit native live PPE audit events through that path.

== Customer discussion

Record one answer for each category:

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Category | Demonstrated today | Customer decision or gap

| Application contract
| OpenAI-compatible request through Praxis
| Required client protocols and provider extensions

| Model serving
| Replaceable RHDP MaaS endpoint
| OpenShift AI, external service, or hybrid model strategy

| Identity and authorization
| OpenShift participant and namespace boundary
| Application, workload, tenant, and policy identities

| Observability
| Exact-request identifier, status, model, and elapsed time
| Metrics, logs, traces, attribution, and retention

| Governance evidence
| Sanitized resource and control inspection
| Policy decisions, OCSF mapping, ledger, and audit workflow
|===

== Define the next proof

Choose one bounded follow-on outcome:

* connect a customer-approved model endpoint through Praxis;
* integrate one representative application without its provider credential;
* demonstrate an approved-model or tenant authorization decision;
* connect the customer's observability platform using exact trace correlation;
  or
* validate a PPE decision-to-evidence contract without model content.

Define the owner, target environment, success evidence, and explicit
out-of-scope items. Avoid a proof of concept that attempts every optional
capability at once.

== Evidence

The customer leaves with a shared distinction between what the lab proved,
what was discussed, and what requires target-environment validation.

== Key takeaway

The useful outcome is a defensible architecture decision and bounded next
proof—not an unsupported production or compliance claim.
